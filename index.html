PythonClaudePostgresOAuth2ISO 27001 ↗ 02 street-coverage-mapper Open source How much of a city have you actually walked? Takes a Google Fit, Apple Health or Strava export, snaps the GPS traces onto the real street graph, and works out which streets you have covered and which you have not. It works for any city because it derives the matching tolerances from the city's own geometry instead of hardcoding them for one place. Falls out to Kepler.gl. PythonGeoPandasOSMnxKepler.gl ↗ 03 portalkit PyPI · Open source One Python API for four incompatible open-data portals. Cities publish their data on Socrata, CKAN, ArcGIS or OpenDataSoft, and each one has a different idea of what a dataset is. portalkit puts a single interface over all four and hands you a pandas or geopandas frame at the end of it, so the boring half of any civic-data project stops being the whole project. PythonpandasgeopandasSocrataCKANArcGIS ↗ 04 PriED Research · Virginia Tech Sharing industrial process data without sharing what the data reveals. Manufacturers want to pool process data and cannot, because the data leaks the process. PriED learns a task-specific VAE-LSTM representation that keeps what a downstream model needs and drops what a competitor would want, with a bilinear-attention search engine on top to pick which shared data is worth using at all. Published at IEEE Big Data 2022; the wider work became my master's thesis. Off the clock

The two things I will talk about for too long.

CricketA statistical argument that never ends

I have written five essays about it and expect to write more. The appeal is not the drama — it is that cricket produces more usable data per hour than almost any other sport, and still cannot settle its own arguments. Averages hide era. Strike rates hide conditions. Everyone knows the numbers and nobody agrees.

Some of that turned into actual work: the ODI piece is a statistical analysis rather than an opinion, and it is the reason I first learned to be careful with a mean.

CitiesUrban planning and public transport

Cities are the largest systems most people ever interact with, and they are legible if you measure the right things — which is why street-coverage-mapper exists, and why the DSPG summer mapping vegetation index against quality of life in Virginia stuck with me more than most of my research.

Mostly it shows up as opinions about frequency over coverage, level boarding, and why a city that measures parking but not headway will keep getting the answer it deserves. Copenhagen has spoiled me.
